Silver lanterns drifted above the quiet harbor while seven curious foxes traded riddles beneath a crooked violet moon at midnight. Across the valley, invisible musicians tuned glass violins, and every note became a tiny blue feather before touching the ground. Maribel packed three oranges, a compass, and an unfinished map, then boarded the morning train without choosing any destination whatsoever.
Inside the last carriage, a sleepy botanist explained why thunderstorms secretly prefer gardens filled with rosemary, moss, and brass statues. Nobody believed him, yet the windows suddenly bloomed with emerald patterns whenever distant clouds rolled over the northern hills slowly. At noon, the conductor served cinnamon tea in porcelain cups decorated with whales, bicycles, comets, and exceptionally polite dragons smiling.
A child wearing yellow boots counted tunnels, whispered predictions, and claimed the thirteenth bridge would lead directly into autumn tomorrow. Instead, the train emerged beside a carnival where mechanical birds sold umbrellas and shadows danced independently behind striped tents nearby. Maribel laughed, purchased a paper crown, and followed a silver cat toward a staircase descending gently into the sky below. There, clocks grew on trees, rivers remembered forgotten names, and every traveler received one bright question to carry home forever.
